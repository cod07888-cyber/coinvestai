Stablecoins Move From Trading Floors to Company Ledgers

📅 Published: August 15, 2026 ✍️ By: CoinvestAI Editorial Desk ⏱️ 7 min read

For most of their history, stablecoins were primarily a trading tool: a way to park crypto profits in a dollar-pegged token between speculative moves. In 2026, that framing is obsolete. A substantial and rapidly growing share of stablecoin activity is now genuine commercial activity — invoices, payroll, supplier settlements, and corporate treasury transfers moving through enterprise finance departments rather than exchange order books.

1. The Shift From Trading to Real Commerce

This structural migration is clearly visible in market metrics. Industry estimates from McKinsey and Artemis Analytics indicate that real commercial stablecoin payment volume—stripped of internal arbitrage, bot activity, and speculative trading—reached roughly $390 billion in 2025, more than double the prior year.

Business-to-business (B2B) stablecoin payments alone climbed to about $221 billion last year, with projections suggesting that figure could comfortably exceed $1 trillion by 2030 if current corporate onboarding rates persist.

2. Why Regulatory Clarity Unlocked Enterprise Adoption

The single greatest barrier for corporate treasuries has historically been legal ambiguity. That fog has largely lifted. The GENIUS Act, enacted in the United States in July 2025, established a robust federal framework governing payment stablecoin reserves, licensing, and consumer redemption guarantees.

Concurrently, the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation has come into full force, imposing strict reserve composition and disclosure mandates. Hong Kong and other key financial hubs have enacted tailored stablecoin licensing frameworks. Compliance departments that once rejected stablecoins outright can now operate within defined regulatory parameters.

3. Infrastructure Races: Cards and Fintechs in Production

Major financial institutions have moved past pilot testing into full commercial deployment. Visa's integration of USDC settlement allows card issuers to settle transactions directly on-chain rather than relying exclusively on legacy banking hours. Mastercard's acquisition of BVNK for approximately $1.8 billion underscores the network's commitment to embedding stablecoin rails into core financial infrastructure.

Meanwhile, Stripe Treasury processed over $220 million in stablecoin transactions across 70+ countries shortly after launch, leveraging multichain routing through networks like Polygon, Ethereum, and Base.

  • H1 2026Polygon payment applications eclipse $10 billion in transaction volume, surpassing all of 2025 combined.

4. Cross-Border B2B and Emerging Markets

Rather than replacing everyday consumer tap-and-pay transactions immediately, stablecoins are solving a acute institutional bottleneck: cross-border business payments. Traditional correspondent banking networks are notoriously slow, expensive, and opaque. Non-G20 cross-border business transactions represent an addressable market exceeding $17.9 trillion, driving intense competition among infrastructure providers.

Analyst Note on Volume Data: Raw on-chain stablecoin transfer volumes reach into the tens of trillions, but analysts emphasize that the vast majority represents trading, wallet rebalancing, and automated bots. Isolating real commercial commerce ($390 billion) provides an accurate baseline for corporate adoption.

5. Friction Points and What Could Slow Down Adoption

Despite rapid momentum, challenges remain. Counterparty trust in tokenized deposits versus traditional bank money varies by jurisdiction. Furthermore, compliance overhead—such as sanctions screening, automated transaction monitoring, and multi-jurisdictional reporting—remains an active operational expense.
