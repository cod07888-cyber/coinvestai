Stablecoins Are Quietly Becoming the Payment Rail Businesses Actually Use

Regulatory clarity has pushed stablecoins into treasury departments. Real commercial payment volume has doubled—and banks are racing to own the rail.

For most of their history, stablecoins were a trading tool. In 2026, that framing is obsolete. A growing share of stablecoin activity is now genuine commercial volume — invoices, payroll, and corporate treasury transfers — moving through enterprise finance departments.

McKinsey and Artemis Analytics estimate that real commercial stablecoin payment volume reached roughly $390 billion in 2025. Business-to-business payments alone climbed to $221 billion, with projections suggesting that figure could pass $1 trillion by 2030.

$390BCommercial volume in 2025 (excluding trading).
$1TProjected B2B volume by 2030.

The Regulatory "Safety" Floor

The regulatory fog has lifted. The US GENIUS Act (2025) and EU MiCA regulation have established the operational boundaries for issuers. CFOs are now integrating these rails into accounting software because the legal risk has been mitigated by clear federal/regional frameworks.

Analyst Note: Raw on-chain volume is often inflated by bots, but the "real commercial" volume is the metric CFOs are watching to justify investment in stablecoin infrastructure.

Conclusion

2026 is the year stablecoins stop being a crypto story and start being a financial utility. The race is now to see which banks and networks will dominate the rails that power tomorrow's corporate global economy.
